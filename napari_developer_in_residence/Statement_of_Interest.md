# Statement of Interest

My motivation for applying to the napari Developer-in-Residence role comes from a **passion for bioimage analysis and for designing microscopy experiments that answer biological questions**. In collaborative projects, I often work with researchers who are experts in their science but do not program every day. Napari has been a valuable common ground: I can make proposed quantifications visible in image layers, labels, and spatial results, so we can examine together whether the quantification captures the observables their hypotheses require. I want to help strengthen the tool that makes this kind of shared reasoning possible.

My foundation is in physics, programming, and the instrumentation and control of microscopes. During my PhD with Dr. Hernan Grecco, **I combined microscopy, image and data analysis, and mathematical modeling of biological systems**. I designed experiments, quantified image-derived observables, and related those measurements to model parameters. My postdoctoral work at Karolinska Institutet deepened this connection between experimental design and quantitative biology. Later, at SciLifeLab and the BioImage Informatics Facility, I supported researchers across microscopy, MRI, and whole-slide imaging, developing practical analysis workflows with biologists and research software developers. I continue this work at the Centro de Microscopias Avanzadas at Universidad de Buenos Aires.

**This work has taught me that an analysis method is useful only when researchers can inspect, understand, and adapt its results**. I have built Python tools and modular workflows for biologists, including GulLiver for whole-slide liver immunostaining, whose OME-Zarr outputs can be explored in napari. I have also used napari and its plugins for segmentation and longitudinal tracking, and taught researchers to use the viewer and pixel-classification tools. In TREX, I collaborated with biologists and research software developers on cloneID filtering and tests ([PR #55](https://github.com/frisen-lab/TREX/pull/55)). These experiences make me especially interested in the roadmap's user-facing core-library work and in architectural improvements that make napari easier to extend and maintain.

**As a practicing microscopist and bioimage analyst, I bring the perspective of someone who uses napari with researchers and is actively engaging with its open-source community**. I participate in the Zulip chat and image.sc, read pull requests to understand the project's design discussions, and have started working on a couple of contributions. I am eager to build on this involvement by contributing well-tested, modular software and clear documentation, while keeping progress visible, seeking design feedback early, and making my work easy for remote collaborators to follow.

The bioimage-analysis community is an important part of why I want to do this work. It has been generous and welcoming to me, and I want to give back by helping other researchers and contributors find their way into the field. **I have participated in AI4Life projects, GloBIAS community work, and LABI, particularly its Image Data Science group**. I also publish reusable, FAIR-minded course materials through Zenodo. These experiences have shown me how shared tools, thoughtful mentorship, and accessible learning resources help people contribute with different backgrounds and levels of programming experience.

**I will bring napari a combination of microscopy experience, Python development, scientific context, and commitment to an inclusive open-source community**. I am excited to help make the core viewer more dependable for the developers who maintain it and more useful to the diverse researchers who rely on it.

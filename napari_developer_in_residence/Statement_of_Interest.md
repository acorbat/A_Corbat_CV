# Statement of Interest

I am applying for the napari Developer-in-Residence role because it brings together the work I find most meaningful: building dependable Python software for scientific images, helping researchers and developers work effectively with it, and improving the foundations of an open-source tool used by a broad community.

My software development has grown out of quantitative imaging research and bioimage analysis. I have a PhD in Physics and have worked with microscopy, whole-slide images, MRI, and other large scientific datasets. In roles at the BioImage Informatics Facility at SciLifeLab and at Universidad de Buenos Aires, I have translated biological and imaging questions into Python workflows, from preprocessing and registration through segmentation, quantification, and visualization. Those projects have taught me that good scientific software is not only an algorithm: it needs understandable interfaces, reproducible data handling, clear documentation, and a path for other people to validate and extend the work.

I have used napari as part of bioimage-analysis workflows, including to inspect OME-Zarr data and segmentation outputs. My public GulLiver project is a practical example of the kind of user perspective I would bring: it is a Python package for whole-slide liver immunostaining images, producing OME-Zarr results that can be inspected in napari. I have not been an upstream napari core contributor, and I would approach the role ready to learn the project's architecture and contribution practices rather than assume familiarity I have not yet earned.

I am especially interested in the roadmap's user-facing core-library work and the architectural effort needed to make the viewer easier to maintain and extend. I would like to contribute to modular, well-tested Python components; improve workflows around large and multidimensional image data; and collaborate with adjacent projects where that strengthens the user experience. I am also drawn to the maintenance and community parts of the role: thoughtful issue triage and review, useful technical documentation, and mentoring contributors so that more people can make progress in the codebase.

My experience teaching practical courses and advising researchers has made me attentive to different levels of technical context. I value concise written updates, explicit questions when blocked, and changes that are easy for collaborators to review. In a remote, asynchronous team, I would make progress visible through regular community participation and well-documented pull requests, while asking for design feedback early on architectural work.

I would bring napari a combination of Python development, scientific-imaging context, experience supporting users, and a strong motivation to grow as an open-source maintainer. I would be glad to help make the core viewer more approachable for both the people who build it and the scientists who depend on it.
